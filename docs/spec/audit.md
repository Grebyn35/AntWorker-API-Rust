# Spec: `audit`

## Purpose

`audit` records who did what: every module writes entries through it, inside the unit of work of the change, and its `SecurityEventSink` records refused requests (D1, D6). A workspace's owner and admins read its log, each the entries whose permission their role holds, with their IP addresses and user agents, and a user reads their own log and every entry of a workspace's log whose target they are, while deleting a user anonymizes the entries that name them (D166, AUDIT-006). An hourly job deletes the entries older than the deployment's retention, and the database keeps `app` from deleting an entry recorded in the last 90 days other than through the foreign keys of a user's or a workspace's deletion (D168). The action catalog, in `openapi.json`, documents every action an entry can hold: what it means, the logs that record it, the kinds of resource it happens to, the permission that reading it in a workspace's log takes, and the schema of its details, which the type that the action declares gives, so no entry holds other details.

## Owns

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| `audit_entries` | `id`; `scope`, `user` or `workspace`; `user_id`, the user whose log holds an entry of the user scope; `workspace_id`, the workspace whose log holds one of the workspace scope; `actor_user_id`, `target_user_id`, `action`, `resource_type`, `resource_id`; `details`, IDs and non-personal fields only; `permission`, which reading an entry of the workspace scope takes beside `audit.read`, or null for one that whoever reads the log reads, and for every entry of the user scope (Reading a workspace's log); `trace_id` and `created_at`. |
| `deletable_audit_entries` | A view of `audit_entries`, which holds no rows of its own: the `id` and `created_at` of each entry recorded more than 2,160 hours, 90 days of 24 hours, before the transaction that reads it began, through which retention deletes entries (AUDIT-005, Retention). |
| `audit_entry_clients` | The client of an entry recorded in a request: `id`, `audit_entry_id`, which is unique; `user_id`, the user whose deletion deletes it: the entry's actor, or, for an entry without one, the user whose log holds it; `client_ip`, which every request has; `user_agent`, null for a request that sent none; and `created_at`. Both `client_ip` and `user_agent` are personal data (DATA-23). |

## Interface

- `record(uow, scope, Entry)`: writes one entry in the given unit of work. The scope names the log and the actor, and comes only from a proof: `Principal::audit_scope`, the user's own log with the user as the actor, or that of `Authorized<P>`, the workspace's log with the member as the actor. `AuditScope::attempt(user_id)` names the log of an account that an unauthenticated request acted on, such as a failed sign-in, or that a subcommand that an operator runs acted on, such as the removal of a second factor (AUTH-072), with no actor. It refuses an entry, as an internal error, unless its action declares both the scope's kind of log and the entry's kind of resource (AUDIT-013).
  `Principal::founder_scope(workspace_id)` names the log of the workspace the caller just created, with them as the actor, before any `Authorized<P>` can name it, and only a user's creation of a workspace may call it, which clippy enforces. <!-- part: workspace_creation -->
  `Principal::invitee_scope(workspace_id)` names the log of the workspace whose invitation the caller accepts or declines, with them as the actor, though they aren't yet, or won't be, its member, so no `Authorized<P>` can name it (INVITATIONS-005, INVITATIONS-017), and only accepting and declining may call it, which clippy enforces. <!-- part: invitations -->
  `AuditScope::setup(workspace_id)` names the log of the workspace that the subcommand `setup-workspace` just created, with no actor, since an operator is no user, and only that subcommand's operation may call it, which clippy enforces (WORKSPACES-023). <!-- part: workspace_setup -->
  `Superadmin::owner_scope(workspace_id)` names the log of the workspace that a superadmin just created, with them as the actor, before any `Authorized<P>` can name it, and only a superadmin's creation of a workspace may call it, which clippy enforces (WORKSPACES-031). <!-- part: superadmins -->
  `Principal::leaver_scope(workspace_id)` names the log of a workspace that the caller leaves as their account is deleted, and only the personal data contract of `workspaces` may call it (ACCOUNTS-014).
- `Retention`, which registers the periodic job `audit.expire_entries`, with the deployment's `AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS` (AUDIT-011).
- `SecurityEventSink`, implemented for `platform::authz`.
  Its `refused_superadmin` records the refusal of a caller who is no superadmin at a superadmin's route (SUPERADMINS-004). <!-- part: superadmins -->
- `Contract`, the personal data contract for `audit_entries` and `audit_entry_clients` (D31), which exports the user's own log and the log of each workspace whose only member is the user, with the client of each entry the user acted in ([accounts spec](accounts.md#what-an-export-holds)), and whose erasure the foreign keys carry out (AUDIT-006).
- `Action<D>` and `Resource`, the types of an entry's action and of the kind of resource it happened to. Each module declares its actions, named `<resource>.<past-tense verb>`, such as `member.removed`, and its kinds of resource, such as `workspace`, as constants in its own `audit.rs`, beside the types of its actions' details, unless another module exports the type, and lists its actions there in `ACTIONS`, which its `mod.rs` exports as `AUDIT_ACTIONS` (ARCH-03). `Action::new(name, description, resources)` declares an action, the sentence that tells what it means, and the kinds of resource it happens to, and the logs that record it follow: `in_own_log()`, a user's own; `in_workspace_log::<P>()`, a workspace's, which shows it only to a reader whose role holds `P`; and `in_workspace_log_for_every_reader()`, a workspace's, which shows it to whoever reads the log (Reading a workspace's log). `D`, `NoDetails` unless the constant's type names another, is the type of its entries' details (The action catalog). Clippy bans these constructors everywhere but a module's `audit.rs`.
- `Entry<D>`: an entry, which `Entry::new(action, resource, id)` builds with the details that `D` holds by default, such as none, `Entry::detailed(action, resource, id, details)` with `details`, and `targeting(target)` gives a target (AUDIT-006). Its details are private to `audit`, so a module sets them only through the type its action declares.
- `Catalog`, the action catalog, which the composition root builds from each module's `AUDIT_ACTIONS`, and whose `document` adds it to the OpenAPI document (AUDIT-012, AUDIT-014).

## Design

### Recording an entry

`record` has the entry admitted to its scope's log, which checks it against its action's declaration (AUDIT-013), then inserts one row through the unit of work's executor, so the entry commits with the change (AUDIT-001), and, in the same statement, the row of its client. The client IP and the user agent come from the unit of work, which opens with its request's (platform spec, "Unit of work"); a job's unit of work holds neither, and records no client, and nor does an operator's subcommand's (AUTH-072). The trace ID is that of the span the entry is recorded in (OBS-07). An `Entry` holds the action, the resource's type and ID, the target user if any, and `details`, of the type that its action declares, a struct of IDs and non-personal fields (SEC-18), which `record` stores as a JSON object; an action of `NoDetails` stores an empty one. An entry that its log's foreign key refuses, since the log's user or workspace went while the change ran, fails the change as an internal error that the refusal causes, so a request whose caller's account went answers as PLATFORM-097 says.

`AuditScope` is a `platform` type whose fields are private: only the proofs build one with an actor, so no module can record an entry as someone the request didn't prove it is (AUDIT-002). A job's run records its entries, of the changes it makes to a module's data (DATA-22), through `JobContext::audit_scope`, in the own log of the user it runs for, with no actor and no client, since no request of theirs made them. An entry of the user scope sets `user_id` and leaves `workspace_id` and `permission` null; one of the workspace scope sets `workspace_id`, and leaves `user_id` null, which the check `audit_entries_scope_check` enforces. An entry of the workspace scope records the permission that its action declares, if any (Reading a workspace's log). `scope` is a Postgres enum (DATA-04), while `action` and `resource_type` are text: each module declares its own actions and resources in its `audit.rs`, so adding one changes no type that every module's entries share.

### Keys and grants

`user_id` references `users` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, since a user's own log belongs to their account. `actor_user_id` and `target_user_id` reference `users` with `ON DELETE SET NULL`, so deleting a user anonymizes the entries of other logs that name them (D6, D31). `workspace_id` references `workspaces` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, since a workspace's log belongs to the workspace, through a migration of its own that follows the one creating `workspaces`. The migration that creates the tables revokes `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on both from `app`, which the first migration's default privileges would otherwise grant, and the one that creates `deletable_audit_entries` revokes `INSERT` and `UPDATE` on it, leaving `app` `SELECT` and `DELETE` (AUDIT-005).

An entry's client IP and user agent belong to its actor, or, for an entry without one, such as a failed sign-in, to the user whose log holds it, and go with the entry, which retention deletes, or with that user's account, whichever goes first. They live in `audit_entry_clients`, whose `audit_entry_id` references the entry with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and whose `user_id` references that user with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, so deleting the user deletes the client of every entry that was theirs, while the entry stays. Postgres carries out a foreign key's action with the privileges of its table's owner, so the deletion of a user, which `app` may make, anonymizes the entries without `app` holding `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on either table, and no request can change an entry any other way (AUDIT-005). A column of the entry itself couldn't go so: a foreign key's action sets to null only its own columns.

Each log's entries are read the latest first through the index on its user, or its workspace, and the entry's time and ID, and the entries whose target is a user through the index on the target and the entry's time and ID (Reading one's own log), each of which indexes its foreign key too (DATA-05).

An entry names a user only in the three columns that reference `users`, or, in that user's own log, which goes with them, as its resource, so these actions leave no entry that names a deleted user (AUDIT-006): an action on a member names the workspace as its resource, and the member as its target, such as `member.removed`, and an action whose resource is a user, such as `user.renamed`, is recorded only in that user's own log.

### Reading a workspace's log

The route authorizes its reader with `audit.read` (D166). The reader's role decides what the page holds, and `access` decides from it (SEC-08): `access::permissions` gives the names of the permissions the role holds, and the query keeps the entries whose `permission` is null or one of them, so the log keeps from a reader what a permission keeps from them, as the workspace's stream keeps its events by the permission each requires (D2, SEC-19), though the log, unlike the stream, holds entries from before the reader joined, refusals, which no event announces, and changes that publish no workspace event, as a workspace's creation. `access::holds::<AuditReadClients>` decides whether the query joins each entry's client (AUDIT-010). Both read the role that the proof carries, which the policy locked for the unit of work (PLATFORM-009), so a page and its reader's authorization agree.

An action that a workspace's log records declares, through `in_workspace_log::<P>()`, the permission of the workspace event that its change publishes, such as `members.read` for `member.removed` and `workspace.transferred`, whose events reach the members who may list members; an action whose change publishes no workspace event, as a workspace's creation, declares `workspace.read`. The tests' support checks it of each request that a test sends with an unsafe method and that succeeds: each entry that the request recorded in a workspace's log with a permission carries the permission of each workspace event that the request published in that workspace, or `workspace.read` when it published none there. `access.denied` declares none, through `in_workspace_log_for_every_reader()`, so whoever reads the log reads a refusal. An entry whose `permission` is null is read with `audit.read` alone, which also covers an entry that a replica of the previous release records while a deploy runs both releases, since its code writes no `permission` (DATA-10).

The query runs through the index on the log's workspace and the entry's time and ID, and the filters narrow the rows it reaches (DATA-17).

### Reading one's own log

A user's own read is the union of two reads, each limited to the page's rows and in the list's order, through its own index: the entries of their own log, by `user_id`, and the entries of the workspace scope whose `target_user_id` is theirs, which no entry of their own log is, so none appears twice. Each filters by the caller's ID alone, which D6 asks of the second: what was done to a user is theirs to see, whatever the workspace (DATA-16). The page joins only the clients whose `user_id` is the caller's, which are the clients of their own log's entries, and of the entries of a workspace's log that they acted in themselves, such as their leaving, as their export holds them (AUDIT-010, the accounts spec's "What an export holds").

### Retention

Retention deletes each entry once it is older than `AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS`, 365 days by default, unless the deletion of its user's account or of its workspace took its log first (D6, D168). The setting takes 90 days at least, the floor of `deletable_audit_entries`, so a deployment's retention never asks to delete an entry that the view keeps, and 3,650 at most, ten years.

The periodic job `audit.expire_entries` runs hourly, and deletes through `deletable_audit_entries` the entries recorded before the retention, in batches of at most 1,000, the oldest first, through the index on `created_at`, until a batch deletes fewer; each entry's client goes with it, through its foreign key (AUDIT-011). It spans every log by nature, so it selects entries by their age alone (DATA-16). It is the only code that names the view: `check-table-ownership` keeps the view's queries to the code of `audit`, and `check-tenancy` keeps them to the functions that it lists, of which the job's `expire` is the one in `audit`, since the view shows no tenant column. No entry's `created_at` ever changes, since `app` updates no entry and a foreign key's action sets only an actor or a target to null, so none that a batch selects becomes younger while the batch waits (DATA-20). Scheduled maintenance isn't audited (DATA-22), and publishes nothing.

`owner` owns the view, which shows each entry recorded more than 2,160 hours before the transaction that reads it began. Both the view and the job count a day as 24 hours, in an interval of hours: Postgres subtracts an interval of days in the session's time zone, which any role may set, and a zone that `app` sets, whose offset changes inside the window, shortens 90 days by as much as two weeks, which no zone does to an interval of hours (AUDIT-005). Postgres deletes through such a view only the rows it shows, with the privileges of its owner on `audit_entries`, so `app`, which holds `DELETE` on the view and none on the table, deletes through it no entry recorded in the last 90 days, whatever statement it runs, and can't replace the view, which it neither owns nor could create (AUDIT-005, DATA-12). A compromised API process can so delete, beside the logs that a user's or a workspace's deletion takes with it, the entries older than 90 days that a retention longer than that still keeps (D168).

A row lock taken through the view asks for `UPDATE` on it, which would let `app` change the entries it shows, so a batch can't skip the rows that another transaction holds, such as an account's deletion, which sets the actor of entries to null. It waits for no lock but the millisecond that is the least `lock_timeout` Postgres takes, as `auth.expire_unfinished` does, so that transaction never waits for the job longer than that while the job waits for it, and the run ends there, rolling its batch back, and leaving the rest to a later run (DATA-20).

### The action catalog

A client learns from `openapi.json` every action that an entry can hold, as it learns every frame of the stream from the event catalog ([realtime spec](realtime.md#the-event-catalog)), and every response of the routes (D8, D170). A later release may record an action that the document a client was built from lacks, which the client passes over (API-18). The catalog is the component `AuditEntry`, a `oneOf` with one schema for each action, in the order of their names (AUDIT-012), each the schema of an entry of that action (AUDIT-014). A client generated from the document so has a type for each action's entries, with their details, and one type that names every action, so it can label each entry by its action, in its users' language, and offer, in a filter of a log's list, the actions and the kinds of resource that the catalog names (AUDIT-009). A list's own items stay `LoggedEntry`, whose `action` is a string, of no set, and whose `details` are an object of any members, so a client reads an entry of an action that its document lacks, such as one that a later release adds (API-18). The catalog comes from the declarations, so it doesn't drift from what the API records: the composition root builds it from each module's `AUDIT_ACTIONS`.
The actions of a part of the template are declared and listed on the part's own lines, so they go with it. <!-- part: removal -->
No route lists the actions: the fork's code alone decides them, so they change only with the document, which a client is built against (D170).

Each action declares what the catalog says of it, and every entry is held to it (AUDIT-013): `repo::insert`, the one statement that writes an entry, takes an `Admitted` entry, which only `Entry::admitted` builds, once the action declares the scope's kind of log and the entry's kind of resource, so neither `record` nor the refusal sink can write an entry without the check. The kinds of log and of resource are checked when the entry is recorded, since an entry's log comes from the proof that names its scope, which no type ties to its action; a declaration that misses a log or a resource fails each test that records such an entry, and SEC-18 asks each action's test to read what it stores. Its details are of its declared type `D`, the one type that `Entry`'s constructors take with its action, in a field private to `audit`, so the compiler keeps any other details out of an entry (AUDIT-014). A type of details holds IDs and non-personal fields only, which review reads in the one place that declares it (SEC-18), such as `ChangedRole`'s `from` and `to`, two roles, or `ThroughProvider`'s `provider`, a provider's key, absent for a sign-in with a password.
A field that only a part of the template sets is declared on the part's own lines. <!-- part: removal -->
Such is `access`, which `access.denied`, `workspace.created` and `workspace.deleted` hold as `superadmin` for a superadmin's refusal, creation or deletion. <!-- part: superadmins -->

An action's component is named after it, `member.removed`'s `MemberRemovedEntry`, as the event catalog names a frame type's, `MemberRemovedFrame`. The catalog's names are action names, which AUDIT-009 shapes, so `check-openapi` reads an entry's `action` as no closed set of API-11. `check-openapi` reads the catalog as the components that `AuditEntry` refers to. It reads the code's actions as text, the name in double quotes that follows each `Action::new(` in a module's `audit.rs`, or in a Rust file of the directory `audit/` that the file becomes once it outgrows its limit (ARCH-03), and it fails on one that no such name follows, as a constant or a raw string would stand there. It fails while the catalog lacks one of those names, whether the module's `ACTIONS` leaves the action out, the composition root leaves out the module's list, or the committed document is older than the action, or lacks a component that the document marks as an action's (AUDIT-012); and while a component's `action` is anything but its action's name, or an action's name isn't one that a log's filter takes (AUDIT-009, AUDIT-014). A declaration that writes its type's parameters before `::new` isn't read, so the check fails on the text `Action::<` in those files. Another spelling that doesn't hold the constructor's text, as through an alias of the type, isn't read either, and review keeps the declarations to the form the check reads. The check fails as well while a schema name that it reads in the code (API-17) is `AuditEntry`, or that of a component that `AuditEntry` refers to or that the document marks as an action's.

### Recording a refusal

A refusal happens before the refused operation has written anything. The `SecurityEventSink` implementation records it in its own short transaction: a member's refusal in the workspace's log, and a non-member's in the caller's own log, so a workspace's log never reveals who probed it. A rank refusal inside an operation reaches the sink through `authz::refuse`, and the operation's own unit of work rolls back (PLATFORM-010). A member's refusal whose workspace's log went with the workspace while the refused request ran (WORKSPACES-017) is refused by the foreign key of the entry's log, `audit_entries_workspace_id_fkey`, which the sink reads by its name (ERR-05): the caller is no member of the workspace any more, so the sink records the refusal in their own log, in a second transaction, as it records a non-member's, with `Refusal::own_scope`. A refusal that goes to its caller's own log, which went with their account meanwhile, such as a read that waited for its own caller's deletion, is recorded nowhere: `audit_entries_user_id_fkey` refuses it, and nothing is left to hold it. Either way the request still answers as refused (AUDIT-004). A member's account may be deleted between the authorization's rollback and the sink's insert, by a deletion that waited at their membership: the foreign key of the entry's actor, `audit_entries_actor_user_id_fkey`, then refuses the entry, which the sink takes as a failure, so the request answers 401 `request.unauthenticated`, as any request does whose insert a deletion of its caller's account left without their row (PLATFORM-097), and its refusal is recorded nowhere.

A caller who is no superadmin, refused at a superadmin's route, is recorded as `access.denied` too, in their own log, as a non-member's refusal is, with the deployment as its resource, which has no ID; a caller whose account went meanwhile has no log left to hold it (SUPERADMINS-004). <!-- part: superadmins -->
